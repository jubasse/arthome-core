# Data model

> Aggregates and invariants per context, duplicated read models and the flow that feeds them,
> persistence. Written for `backend-contracts` and for the porting of `@arthome/core`
> (milestone 1).
>
> A reading rule: **`shared/` is authoritative on the rules and the vocabulary, not on the
> shapes.** Every time a fixture is a mockup convenience, it is said so.

---

## 0. Conventions that hold everywhere

| Subject | Rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| instants | `timestamptz`, **always UTC**, never an offset in minutes (D7) | a date scheduled six months out displays at the wrong hour after a daylight-saving change |
| time zone | an **IANA** identifier (`Europe/Paris`) on the venue, never `utcOffsetMin` (D3) | same |
| offset | **served, recomputed at every serve**, for that date's instant | `storefront-mobile`: bundling a time zone database is expensive across five applications. The computation happens **once**, server-side: the "no value computed twice" rule is respected |
| amounts | `bigint` in **whole minor units** + `char(3)` ISO currency code | never a formatted string stored or transported, except inside an invoice |
| rounding | `roundMinor()` in `@arthome/core`, **to the minor unit, on each component taken separately** | that is what `shared/` carries and what has authority |
| identifiers | **UUIDv7 generated by the domain** — see §7.1 | the aggregate knows its identifier before insertion, which the outbox requires and which the studio's draft requires |
| vocabularies | enumerations **typed from `@arthome/core`**, never hard-coded in a service or in an application | E2: the project's dominant fault, committed on eight fields by five mockups |
| **two vocabularies MAY share a value** | and twenty of the 182 already do — `cancelled` sits in four, `none` in four, `scheduled` in three. The field name disambiguates, not the value | see the note below: I asserted the opposite, and it was false |
| **a vocabulary's spelling on the wire** | **one spelling, everywhere**, and `code-conventions.md` §5.2 has ruled which: **`snake_case`**. A Protobuf enumeration name is `UPPER_SNAKE` by `buf`'s rule; **that is a symbol name, not a wire value**, and the correspondence is mechanical | an `opens.includes('multi-screen')` against a payload carrying `multi_screen` returns `false` **in silence**: everybody falls back to one screen. That is the exact shape of E1. Note that this argument is about **divergence**, not about which separator wins: it holds identically under either spelling, and §5.2 settled the separator by counting the wire rather than by preference |

> **A reversal recorded, and the conversion done in two halves.** This row previously said the wire
> spelling was **`shared/`'s, to the letter** — hence `kebab-case`. `code-conventions.md` §5.2
> reversed it by counting the 374 distinct members declared across the two contracts: **125
> `snake_case` against 12 `kebab-case`**. The criterion is which side is expensive to change —
> published enum values consumed by five surfaces and seven services cannot be renamed later, the
> domain's internal spelling can — and it points at the wire. The reversal is better argued than
> what it replaced, and my own E1 argument above never depended on the separator.
>
> **Measured before converting — and then measured again, because the first measurement was
> taken against the wrong text.** `@arthome/core` declared **95** multi-word members. My first pass
> compared them to the contracts by grepping the YAML, concluded that **13 were still spelled
> `kebab-case` on the wire**, and held them as needing a contract edit and a second owner's
> decision.
>
> **They did not.** Grepping the whole document matched those values in **descriptions and prose**;
> parsing only the `x-arthome-vocabulary:` blocks — which is where a vocabulary actually lives — the
> wire was `snake_case` throughout and had been all along. Nothing needed coordinating. The
> thirteen were core being wrong on its own, and they were held for a week on a measurement
> artefact.
>
> **The lesson is the one this file keeps relearning: assert against the structure, never against a
> transcription.** `adr-auth.md` §5.1 says to read `SEAT_CODE_ALPHABET` from the import and never
> from a copy. Grepping a contract's prose instead of parsing its vocabulary blocks is the same
> move, one level out.
>
> **All 95 are converted, and core-vs-wire separator divergence is 0.** That includes the seven
> members whose spelling comes from `shared/` — `all_lives`, `free_dates`, `multi_screen`, `no_ads`,
> `one_live_month`, `read_only`, `replay_online` — under D-034: `shared/` → `@arthome/core` is a
> **one-time port** that already normalises by design, while `core` ↔ the wire is a **live
> boundary**, and only a live boundary turns a mapping into a parallel table with a codec's costume.
>
> **One of them was a live defect, not a style question.** Core exported `'read-only'` while both
> contracts carried `read_only`, so `mode === ChatMode.READ_ONLY` was `false` **in silence** on
> every payload — on the vocabulary that gates what a viewer may do in a live chat. That is the
> `multi_screen` authorisation defect a second time, inside the package written to prevent it.
> `hold-screen` and `co-production` were the same class: a defect found once is rarely alone.

> **A premise of mine that was empirically false, and a ruling was built on it (D-035).**
> I argued that two closed vocabularies **cannot** share a value here, "since `arthome-check-enums`
> discovers values rather than vocabularies". Measured against the code: **twenty values of 182 are
> already shared across two or more of the 44 vocabularies, and the gate is green on every one.**
> `cancelled` is in four, `none` in four, `scheduled` and `interrupted` in three.
>
> The gate maps a value to the *first* vocabulary that declares it and reports copies **outside the
> declaring files**. Two declarations were never in conflict. I had inferred the constraint from the
> gate's name instead of reading what it does — the same fault as asserting against a transcription
> rather than against the structure.
>
> **What it cost:** a family of disambiguation suffixes — `none_sanction`, `run_ended`,
> `scheduled_payout`, `manual_cause`, `incident_*`, `crew_*`, `pane_*`, `*_order` — that existed to
> avoid a collision that was never real. They are gone, and the bare forms now collide happily:
> `CREW_ROLES` and `MEMBER_ROLES` share all four of `director`, `video`, `sound`, `moderation`,
> which is exactly the case I said could not exist.
>
> **What survives it:** the two vocabularies are still two vocabularies, and the distinction is the
> one already written above — a crew role is held on ONE date, a member role in the channel. The
> disambiguation now lives where it belongs, in the **field name** (`crewRole` against `roles`),
> which is a fact about the wire rather than about a gate. `moderation_page` keeps its suffix for
> that reason and that reason only: `roles: [moderation]` and `navigation: [moderation]` would
> otherwise sit in one `EffectiveRights` payload meaning two different things.

**Nullability.** Every optional field is declared nullable explicitly, and the contract tells
**"not measured"** apart from **"measured as zero"** (`streaming.md` makes it a rule of honesty: a
zero reads as *perfect*, not as *not measured*). That holds for every feed metric, and for the
viewer counter, **absent** — never zero — when the date is not on air.

## 1. `identity`

### 1.1 `Account` — root aggregate

```
Account
  id                 uuid v7, never exposed
  public_handle      citext unique, exposed ("@marie.j") — see §7.1
  email              citext unique, + email_verified_at
  phone              nullable, + phone_verified_at
  password_hash      argon2id, nullable (social-only account)
  totp_secret        encrypted, nullable      backup_codes    hashed
  passkeys           child table
  member_number      generated, displayed
  status             active | suspended | deletion_requested | anonymised
  created_at · updated_at · version
```

**Invariants.** An account with no `password_hash` must have at least one social provider or one
passkey. `deletion_requested` freezes purchases but deletes nothing (§7.5).

### 1.2 `Profile` — entity of the `Account` aggregate

Up to five per account (a television constraint, but carried by the account). Name, sized avatar,
`kind` (`adult | child`), and for a child profile the **list of allowed disciplines**. Filtering the
child catalogue happens **server-side**: otherwise a child's TV downloads the adult catalogue in
order to hide it.

### 1.3 `Device` and `DeviceSession` — two aggregates, not one

The cut adopted is `adr-auth.md` §4/Q3's, which resolves E13 (`devices` has two shapes under one
name) and `storefront-mobile`'s inconsistency 11:

- **`Device`** — the **registered** device, durable, revocable. Its identifier is obtained at first
  launch, **before any session**. Kind, label, city derived from the address, `last_seen_at`, a
  nullable `push_token` (delegated to `notifications`, only the link here).
- **`DeviceSession`** — the **(device, profile)** pair. A living-room television carries up to five:
  that is what gives meaning to "the other accounts stay signed in".

**Invariants — and both acts publish, each at its own grain.**

| Act | Publishes | What `streaming` revokes |
|---|---|---|
| **disconnect a profile** (`signOutProfile`) | `identity.device_session.closed.v1` (carries **`profile_id`**) | the leases of the **(device, profile)** pair, and of those alone — "the other accounts stay signed in" |
| **revoke the device** (`revokeDevice`) | `identity.device.revoked.v1` | **all** of the device's leases |

**The second event was missing, and its absence emptied the cut of its meaning.** `DeviceRevoked`
carries only `device_id`: so `streaming` had no way to learn that a profile had been disconnected,
and the `PlaybackSession(account, profile, device, date)` lease kept renewing. On the shared
television that is the **very reason** for the `Device` / `DeviceSession` distinction, "disconnect
this profile" stopped no playback — and since the screen ceiling is 1 outside `premium`, you stayed
locked out of your own account, the exact defect `storefront-mobile` Q5 and `storefront-tv` Q9c
asked us to avoid.

**Exposure window in both cases: up to 120 s** — the lifetime of the token already in hand, not the
renewal interval (`adr-stream-entitlement.md` §3.3).

### 1.4 `DevicePairing` — root aggregate

```
DevicePairing
  id · device_id · profile_id (nullable for `signin`)
  intent          signin | seat | plan | payment_method | merch
  user_code       char(6), alphabet declared by adr-auth.md §5.1 (no confusables)
  payload         jsonb, opaque to identity, relayed to the target service
  state           pending | approved | denied | expired | cancelled | approved_with_failure
  approved_by_account_id   nullable
  expires_at · created_at · version
```

**Invariants.**
- `user_code` is unique **among `pending` pairings only** — a partial index
  `WHERE state = 'pending'`. A code is reused after expiry, otherwise the space is exhausted.
- One single transition out of `pending`. A second call on a settled pairing returns the original
  outcome, never an error (idempotence: both the TV and the phone may retry).
- The attempt counter is per `user_code` **and** per `device_id`, not per IP address.
- The intent's result (seat created, subscription, payment method) **is not stored here**:
  `identity` relays the `payload` to the target service, which executes and publishes. The pairing
  carries the reference, and `PairingOutcome` is composed by the BFF. Otherwise `identity` would
  know about ticketing.

### 1.5 `Channel` — root aggregate (the channel as a **workspace**)

```
Channel
  id · owner_account_id · verified_at (nullable) · created_at · version
  members[]        ChannelMembership
  invitations[]    ChannelInvitation
```

```
ChannelMembership
  id · channel_id · person_id
  roles          set<MemberRole>   ← a SET, never a single role
  is_owner       bool              ← never removable
  joined_at · note
```

**The `MemberRole` vocabulary, eight values, `catalogue.json`'s**:
`artist · production · coordination · director · video · sound · moderation · treasury`.
The fold onto six personas is **a label**, it appears in no response (E6).

**Invariants.**
- `is_owner = true` on exactly one row; it is never deletable and its roles are never editable.
  `transferOwnership` moves the flag, and requires the recipient to be **already a member** and to
  have **two-factor authentication**.
- `inviteMember(role)` is allowed only if `role ∈ assignableRolesOf(roles)`, the projection of
  `grants` onto the roles held. Otherwise a refusal with the code and **the list of roles
  assignable from this level**, and who to ask.
- Deleting a channel is refused while a date remains on sale or a payout is due — facts projected
  from `ticketing` and `payouts`, kept locally, never asked for synchronously.

### 1.6 `DateAccessGrant` — root aggregate (the one-off stand-in)

Scoped to **one date**, with an **explicit and served** `expires_at` ("expires at curtain-down + 1 h"
is a screen sentence; the contract carries the instant). `crew_role`
(`director | video | sound | moderation`), never a full `MemberRole`. Revocable without touching
membership of the channel.

**A structuring invariant**: assigning the `director` slot is what **gives access to the stream
key**. So it is reserved to `artist ∨ production`, and the contract makes that reason explicit
rather than leaving it to be guessed.

### 1.7 `Person` — the directory

Professional identity: name, city, freelance or not, roles held, `channels[]`, `runs_called`.
**`runs_called` is a read model**, not a hand-written field: it increments on
`streaming.run.ended.v1`.

### 1.8 Preferences, consents, relations

- `AccountPreferences` — **account scope**: interface language, default subtitles, audio
  description, behaviour when opening a live show (`peek | muted | off`), chat open/closed,
  **display** currency, **reading time zone**.
- `DevicePreferences` — **device scope**: default quality, subtitle size, reduced motion, automatic
  video preview, run-desk layout, named encoding profiles.

  **The answer to `storefront-tv` Q8 and `storefront-mobile` §14: two scopes, and the contract says
  so field by field.** Subtitle size and reduced motion depend on the television and the room;
  language and time zone depend on the person. A single scope would be wrong half the time. The
  studio's reading time zone is **the same field** as the storefront's: same account, one owner
  (`studio-mobile` inconsistency 9).

  **The preferences resource is additive and tolerant**: a key unknown to one version of the
  application is neither rejected nor erased on the next write. Otherwise the mobile version under
  store review overwrites settings made from the studio web. The write is **field by field**, never
  a whole document.
- `Consent` — four purposes (`audience`, `perso`, `partners`, `ads`) and two tracker categories.
  **Timestamped by the server and versioned with the version of the text accepted**: a consent with
  no version and no date is worth nothing. `ads` is `false` by default, and **that default is a
  contract decision**, not a setting.
- `ArtistFollow`, `WatchlistEntry` — flat, declarative relations. **They are state settings, not
  toggles**: two sends of the same "follow" leave one follow; a toggle on a doubtful network
  inverts the result.

  **Does `followArtist` create a notification subscription?** (`storefront-tv`). No: two distinct
  settings. `ArtistFollow` is a catalogue relation; the alert is a flag **per followed artist**
  carried by `notifications`. The mobile mockup shows the two separately, and conflating them would
  make it impossible to follow an artist without being notified.

## 2. `catalog`

### 2.1 `Show` — root aggregate

Title and synopsis **in both languages where they exist**, cast, discipline, **list of sub-genres**
(§2.6), tags, running time, `languageDependency`, artwork.

### 2.2 `Date` — root aggregate

```
Date
  id · show_id · venue_id · channel_id
  slug                  stable, per language (§2.7)
  starts_at             timestamptz UTC
  runtime_min           denormalised from the show (it freezes at publication)
  rights                scope (worldwide | restricted) · territories[] · reason_code
  replay_policy         included | subscription | unit | none
  replay_window_hours   int
  outcome               nullable: postponed | cancelled | interrupted
  rescheduled_to        nullable timestamptz
  outcome_declared_at · outcome_declared_by
  created_at · updated_at · version
```

**Invariants.**
- `replay_policy = 'none'` is **final** for this date: you cannot later enable a replay you promised
  not to make — the public price depended on it. The other values lock when the box office opens.
- `outcome` is never rewritten nor erased: a declared outcome is a fact.
- `rescheduled_to` exists only if `outcome = 'postponed'`.
- `reason_code` is a **code** (`co_production | broadcaster | festival`), never a sentence. The
  current data carries `label`/`labelEn` written into it: that is an i18n leak (E8).

**What is not on this table, and what the fixture puts there**: `prices`, `seats`, `revenue`,
`sold`, `viewers`, `chatMode`, `publication`, `publishedBy`. Copying the fixture's shape would carve
a read model into the write contract.

### 2.3 `Publication` — root aggregate

```
Publication
  id · date_id · channel_id
  state           draft | reserve | scheduled | technical | live | ended | replay-online
  order_rank      int, served with the state  ← the studio's sort by state follows the machine, not the alphabet
  published_at · prices_locked_at · replay_online_at
  checklist       7 items, each with its state and its source
  last_actor_id · last_actor_surface · version
```

**The machine, and what moves it forward.**

| From → to | Trigger | One-way |
|---|---|---|
| `draft → reserve` | studio command | no |
| `reserve → draft` | studio command | — |
| `draft \| reserve → scheduled` | studio command | **yes** — *publishing commits the displayed price* |
| `scheduled → technical` | studio command | no |
| `technical → scheduled` | studio command | — |
| `technical → live` | **`streaming.run.started.v1` consumed** | — |
| `live → ended` | **`streaming.run.ended.v1` consumed** | — |
| `ended → replay-online` | studio command, guarded | **yes** — *viewers have paid for the replay* |

**This point is the answer to "an aggregate straddling three contexts".** `Publication` does not
command going on air: it **learns** it. The "go on air" command goes to `streaming`, which alone
knows whether the feed is coming in. So two of the eight transitions are caused by an event, and
`Publication` stays a single context's aggregate.

**Invariants.**
- The lock is on the **pair** `from > to`, not on the state (E5). Refusal:
  `TRANSITION_IRREVERSIBLE` + the target transition + the promise committed, as parameters.
- Every transition is **conditioned on the version**: sent from `technical` while the current state
  is `live`, it is refused with `STATE_CONFLICT` **and the current state and version**. The studio
  is multi-operator with no lock: the arbitration is on the server.
- Every transition carries an `Idempotency-Key` — **mandatory, not recommended**: it commits a
  public price or a sale.
- The transitions offered are served **for this operator**, computed by
  `nextPublicationTransitions(state, effectiveRights)`. Otherwise each surface recomputes the table.

**The checklist, NINE items, seven of them blocking, and three come from elsewhere:**

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| `title_and_discipline`, `poster`, `description` | `catalog`, its own fields |
| `at_least_one_active_price`, `capacity` | **projected** from `ticketing.date_sales.pricing_changed.v1` and `ticketing.date_sales.capacity_set.v1` |
| `technical_check_passed` | **projected** from `streaming.run.technical_check_passed.v1` |
| `chat_mode_set` | **projected** from `chat.date_chat_policy.changed.v1` |

Served as a **list of missing identifiers**, never a percentage. "Chapters planned" and "moderator
assigned" become **non-blocking warnings**: it must be possible to publish a date without chapters.

### 2.4 `Venue`, `Artist`, `Taxonomy`

`Venue`: name, city, country, region, **IANA zone**, capacity, venue type. `utcOffsetMin` disappears
(D3).

`Artist`: the **public face** of the channel, 1:1 with `identity.Channel` by `channel_id`. Name,
bilingual biography, avatar, discipline, country, joining date. `followers` and `avg_viewers` are
**projected counters** (§4), with a declared freshness: a counter wrong by 3% does not matter, a
counter that differs between the sheet and the list does.

`Taxonomy`: universes, disciplines, sub-genres, tags, attribute groups, and the **editorial rank**
(`rank`), which is authoritative — **no surface reorders**. Served as an **immutable versioned
artefact**, per language and per surface, not as an API table. The authoritative counts and
vocabulary are in `prototypes/shared/taxonomy.json`, and nowhere else.

### 2.5 `SavedSearch` — root aggregate

```
SavedSearch
  id · account_id
  scope           search | category           category_id nullable
  name            free text, nullable
  query_text · criteria (normalised jsonb) · criteria_version (int) · criteria_signature (text)
  tab · sort · channels[] (push | email | in_app) · active · created_at
```

**Three invariants that answer the surfaces' three needs.**
1. **Filter values are stable identifiers**, never array indices. The mockup filters on
   `fCats: [1]` — a position. A position survives neither a shareable URL, nor a saved search, nor
   the insertion of a discipline.
2. **`criteria_version`**: when the filter grammar changes, yesterday's search **still runs** if
   migration is possible, otherwise it marks itself `stale` and says so. It never vanishes in
   silence. The answer to `storefront-web` Q24.
3. **`criteria_signature`** is produced by `normalizeSearchCriteria()` in `@arthome/core` — once,
   never client-side. It is what answers "already saved" on two screens and what deduplicates at
   write time.

**The match counter** (`storefront-web` Q23): neither realtime, nor recomputed every time the
Account page is displayed. **"New since your last visit"**, held by a counter incremented by the
*percolator* when a new date matches, reset to zero on read. Ten searches per account then cost
**zero** counting queries when the page opens. The two other options cost ten aggregations per
render, for information whose accuracy nobody will measure.

### 2.6 The sub-genre is **multiple** (E9, `storefront-web` inconsistency 5)

`taxonomy.json` declares it "optional, multiple", `catalogue.json` carries it in the singular, and
the web search filter is a multi-select. **The contract settles it: `genre_ids[]`.** A show that is
both "contemporary" and "repertoire" exists; the singular forbade it.

And **`attributes` in fact carries tags**: `revival`, `new-creation`, `opening-night`, `open-air`,
`archive` are *tags* in `tagPolicy`'s sense, not values of the seven attribute groups. A name
collision between two notions: the contract separates them into `tag_ids[]` and `attributes{}`.

### 2.7 The canonical URL and the slugs

**There is no canonical URL for a date**, and that is a first-order gap: it is what gets shared,
what gets bookmarked, what a reminder and a notification point at, what gets indexed. The web
mockup addresses the page by artist and resolves "the current date" on arrival — so sharing,
reminders and search indexing all point at a target that changes.

**The contract carries**: `Date.slug` and `Show.slug`, stable, **per language** (`slug_fr`,
`slug_en`), and a **served `canonical_url`**, never built by the surface. It is what the TV encodes
in a QR for the Share action (`storefront-tv` Q10, E15), and it is what the web uses for its
cross-language alternate links.

## 3. `ticketing`

### 3.1 `DateSales` — root aggregate (a date's commercial face)

```
DateSales
  date_id (root) · channel_id · currency · market_id   ← the BILLING market: the selling
                                                          currency, NEVER a tax notion
  capacity_total · capacity_tiers[]  (opened by tiers, never shrunk after going on sale)
  seats_available · waitlist_count
  price_tiers[]     full | reduced | support, amount in minor units
  service_fee_rule  a served schedule, not a screen constant
  promotions[]      reason · struck price · current price · validity window
  replay_unit_price nullable, when replay_policy = 'unit'
  prices_locked_at  nullable
  complimentaries[] issued / allocated, by category
  technical_provision  threshold, provisioning, revision deadline, penalty exposure
  version
```

**Invariants.**
- Capacity **widens by tiers, never shrinks** after going on sale.
- `seats_available` never goes below zero: the decrement and the creation of the seat are **in the
  same transaction**. It is the system's hardest concurrency invariant.
- **Opening a tier notifies the waiting list in the same act**: one single transactional command,
  with the **priority window (2 h) as a domain parameter**. Two calls would let the scarcity
  dissipate between them.
- Beyond **10,000 seats**, the infrastructure is provisioned in advance; a forecast far above the
  real figure incurs a penalty; revisable up to **72 h** before. **Threshold, provisioning, deadline
  and exposure are contract data**, not constants copied onto five surfaces (`studio-web` Q25,
  `studio-mobile` #13).
- Prices lock when the box office opens (`publication.engaged` consumed); the schedule locks when
  the show goes on air.
- "Apply to the series" **excludes prices and capacity**: each date commits its own buyers.

**The price paid is not the tier's price.** The summary is composed **server-side**:
`tier + service fee − subscription discount − promotion = total`. All four lines come from the
contract. It is exactly the "a total composed in two places" case the file cites as a typical
defect.

**The answers this gives**:
- `storefront-web` Q11 — **service fees are per seat**, and the schedule is served. The storefront
  web shows a "service fee" line in its summary: it must be computable only once.
- `storefront-web` Q12 — the subscription discount on seats (`seatDiscount`, 10% / 20%) and the shop
  discount (15%) are **two discounts on two bases**; they are not the same (E1). **They do not stack
  with a promotion**: we apply **the one most favourable to the viewer**, and that rule is in
  `@arthome/core`. Otherwise it will be written three times.
- `storefront-web` Q10 — the price sent with the purchase is **verified**; the refusal has a code
  **distinct** from a payment failure: `PRICE_STALE`, with the current price as a parameter. With
  five promotion reasons, one of them computed pro rata of the time elapsed, the gap between the
  price displayed and the valid price is **structural, not accidental**.
- The "show already started" price (`late_rate`) is **pro rata of the time remaining**: it cannot be
  a frozen string. The contract carries **the rule and its parameters**, and serves the current
  price with its `validUntil` (60 s).

### 3.2 `SeatHold` — the capacity hold, and its duration

Raised by `auth` and it commits `ticketing`: **the duration of a `seat` pairing must be the duration
of a seat `hold`**, otherwise the capacity shown on the TV is wrong for the whole time of the wait.
The case is concrete: the TV shows "12 seats", the viewer goes to fetch their phone, and for five
minutes nothing guarantees those twelve seats still exist.

```
SeatHold
  id · date_id · account_id · profile_id · tier · quantity
  origin        checkout | pairing        origin_ref  (checkout session | pairing_id)
  expires_at    timestamptz
  state         active | consumed | expired | released
```

**The invariant, and it is the answer:**

> **`SeatHold.expires_at` is the SAME instant as the expiry of the purchase intent that created
> it.** One instant, carried by both objects, never two durations that drift apart.

| Origin | Intent | The hold's `expires_at` |
|---|---|---|
| web or mobile payment | the checkout session | **15 min** |
| TV pairing, `intent = seat` | `DevicePairing` | **5 min** — the pairing's |

The hold is placed **when the pairing opens**, not at its approval: it is at the moment the TV shows
the code that the capacity must become true. And that **justifies, after the fact, the 5 min
duration adopted for `seat`** (`adr-auth.md` §4/Q4): a pairing duration is a capacity commitment,
and fifteen minutes of commitment per hesitant viewer would empty a popular venue without a single
seat being sold.

**Two consequences `backend-contracts` must carry:**

1. the `seats_available` served publicly is **net of active holds** — otherwise two viewers buy the
   last seat;
2. a hold's expiry republishes `ticketing.date_sales.availability_changed.v1`, so the capacity
   **goes back up** on every surface without any of them asking for anything.

### 3.3 `Seat` — an entity, and `SeatOrder` — a root aggregate

```
Seat
  id · date_id · account_id · profile_id (nullable) · tier
  seat_code       issued by the SERVER                   ← never derived client-side
  state           held | active | cancelled | refunded | transferred | credited
  cancel_deadline timestamptz, served                    ← never the sentence "up to 1 h before"
  order_id
```

**The seat code is issued by the server** (`storefront-web` Q18). It appears identically on the web,
on mobile and on TV. The mockup computes it by hashing: ported as it stands, it would give **three
different codes for the same seat** as soon as one surface changed hash function. A served format,
never recomposed.

### 3.4 `MerchOrder` — root aggregate, and `MerchItem`

`MerchItem`: identifier, show, channel, **bilingual** label (E10: `merchPool` has no `labelEn`, a
data gap to fill at porting time), kind, **variants** (a t-shirt with no size cannot be sold —
`storefront-web` Q17), price, stock, state (`on_sale | out_of_stock`), **source**
(`arthome | shopify | woocommerce | prestashop | drupal | api`), and the merchant address for
external sources.

**An invariant, returned in §context-map 1.3: a `MerchOrder` is single-seller.** A basket spanning
two channels splits into two orders at payment.

**`ExternalOrder` — a distinct aggregate, and that matters.** An order placed on the artist's own
shop is a **read-only reflection**: no invoice, no tracking, no refund on our side, and the contract
owns that explicitly rather than serving empty fields. It carries `external_ref`, `external_host`,
`state: 'external'` (an opaque vocabulary), `synced_at` and `sync_source`. **What we guarantee**:
freshness as of `synced_at`, nothing more; when the external host does not answer, the reflection is
served with its age, not as an error. The answers to `storefront-web` Q16 and `storefront-mobile`
Q9.

### 3.5 `Cart` — root aggregate

**The basket lives on the account, not on the browser** (`storefront-web` Q13, `storefront-mobile`
Q8). Reasons: the mockup shows a persistent basket in the header, it is built up across several
sessions from a live show's shop, and all three storefronts show it. A local basket would survive
neither a reinstall nor a change of device, and we would have to tell the viewer so.

Conflict between two devices: **per line, last writer wins, and the rank comes from the server** —
a version number, never a date from the phone (whose clock drifts and jumps).

**The quote is binding** (`storefront-web` Q14): `CartQuote` carries subtotal, shipping, discount,
total and **`valid_until` (15 min)**. The total presented is the one that will be charged. Beyond
that, a new quote. Shipping is computed **at the quote**, not when adding to the basket.

### 3.6 `Subscription` and `Plan`

`Plan`: the authoritative vocabulary is `catalogue.json`'s — **`free` (0), `pass` (12),
`premium` (24)**, with `opens[]` (nine values: `browse`, `trailers`, `free_dates`, `replays`,
`no_ads`, `one_live_month`, `all_lives`, `multi_screen`, `archive`) and `seat_discount`.

**E1 is the gravest divergence in the file, and it must be stated as such.** Four disjoint
vocabularies coexist, `helpers.planOf()` falls **all** accounts back to `free`, and `plan.opens[]`
conditions access to playback. **That is an authorisation defect, not a display defect.** The
contract fixes one single set, and tells what is a **plan** (`free`, `pass`, `premium`) apart from
what is a **way of buying** (the one-off seat, which is not a subscription). `monthly`, `season`,
`none` are removed from the vocabulary: no data references them.

`Subscription` **exists nowhere in `shared/`** — the mockup shows it as a literal. Shape to create:
`plan_id`, `state` (`active | past_due | cancelled | trialing`), `started_at`,
`current_period_end`, `payment_method_ref`, `cancel_at_period_end`, invoices.

**`multi_screen` is an execution constraint, not a marketing line**: "two screens at once" imposes a
server-side count, held by `streaming` (§5.4). `ticketing` publishes the ceiling; `streaming`
enforces it.

### 3.7 `Credit` — the account credit note

`storefront-web` spotted it: the credit appears in the copy and nowhere else in the file. It is **an
internal currency**, therefore a liability, therefore an aggregate.

```
Credit
  id · account_id · channel_id (scope)  · amount_minor · currency
  origin        interrupted_date | goodwill
  origin_ref    date_id
  state         issued | partially_used | used | expired
  expires_at    12 months
```

**The recommended scope, and it is a decision to confirm**: a credit is issued for an `interrupted`
outcome and is **redeployable on the same channel only**. An accounting reason, explained in
`adr-payments.md` §6: a credit used elsewhere would force the platform to fund another artist's
share out of its own money. The restriction is reversible; ignoring it is not.

## 4. The duplicated read models, and the flow that feeds them

This is where the event-driven architecture earns its place: **no screen is served by a join at
query time.** Every model below is a denormalised table, written by an idempotent consumer, read in
one query.

| `inbox` | `notifications` | — | every context, routed by role |

**`entitlement_projection` is the only projection that carries AUTHORITY.** The table above counts
seven others, and they are legitimate: `date_card_public`, `date_detail_public`, `channel_agenda`,
`events_table`, `artist_counters`, `channel_dues`, `payout_ledger`, plus the three projected items
of the publication checklist. **The distinction is not the number, it is the nature**: the seven
others feed a **display**, and a display five seconds out of date corrects itself;
`entitlement_projection` decides an **entitlement**, and a stale entitlement issues a token. That is
why it alone carries a numbered freshness budget (≤ 5 s) and an alert. An earlier version of this
paragraph said "the only duplication in the system" — that was false, and the sentence would have
served to refuse the eighth legitimate projection. `streaming` keeps a copy of seat holdings, of the
subscription and of the date's state, because it is the only one that can decide an entitlement and
the only one that issues a token. The alternative would be a synchronous call between services —
forbidden — or an entitlement decided by the BFF, which has no authority. The freshness tolerated is
≤ 5 s; beyond that, `context-map.md` §11's alert fires.

**Every read model carrying a date carries `displayState` and `displayStateValidUntil`** —
storefront **and studio**, without exception. The value is produced by `displayStateOf` in
`@arthome/core` (`context-map.md` §5); no surface composes `publication.state`, `run.state` and
`outcome` itself. The studio is the case that matters most: it is the one with the three axes, and
its outcome labels **replace** the state. `order_rank` is still served alongside for **sorting** by
state, which is a different need.

**The lag is bounded and visible.** Every read model carries **a monotonic version and the instant
it was last applied** — the two facts, and deliberately not two fixed column names. What is fixed is
the **wire** name: `@arthome/contracts` declares `lastEventSeq` on both envelope metas, and declares
it `.nullable().optional()` — which is what lets a model whose version is per-document rather than a
per-model stream position serve `null` instead of inventing a number it does not hold.
**No column called `last_event_seq` or `last_event_at` exists anywhere in the platform**;
`search-indexer`'s `show_projection` carries `version` and `indexed_at` and satisfies the invariant
in full (`definition-of-done.md` §5.1, S4). The BFF serves `servedAt` and the contract declares the
freshness per family:

| Family | Freshness guaranteed |
|---|---|
| taxonomy, disciplines | immutable artefact, 24 h |
| `category`, `artist`, `plans`, `account` | 5 min |
| `home`, `tickets`, `list`, `replays` | 60 s |
| `live`, capacity, viewer counter | 15 s |
| `PlaybackTicket`, entitlement verdict | **never cached** |

This table answers `storefront-tv` (freshness per read model) and `storefront-web` Q7.

## 5. `streaming`

### 5.1 `Run` — root aggregate

```
Run
  id · date_id · channel_id
  state           idle | rehearsal | on_air | interrupted | ended
  ingest_protocol rtmps | srt | whip          ← decides which metrics are available
  monitor_path    whep | ll_hls               ← decides the latency promised to the operator
  quality_ladder[]  renditions enabled/disabled
  cameras · started_at · ended_at · version
```

**`run.state` loses `postponed` and `cancelled`**: they were echoes of `outcome` lodged in the
technical axis. A run desk has no "cancelled" state; it has a stage sending nothing.

**Invariants.** `idle → on_air` is refused if the technical check never passed. Going on air
publishes `streaming.run.started.v1`, which `catalog` consumes to advance the publication. **A grace
period when going offline**: a two-second network break at the venue produces neither an incident
nor an HLS manifest restarted from zero. The state pushed to the studio is the state **after** the
glitch has been absorbed, and the studio tells "glitch absorbed" apart from "publisher gone" — two
fields, not one.

### 5.2 `StreamKey` — root aggregate

**A secret displayed on a phone, in a venue, often in front of a contractor.** The contract
guarantees four things (`studio-mobile`):
- the key **is never in a list payload**;
- revealing it is a **distinct, audited and by-name command**;
- rotating it is immediate and **the old one stops broadcasting at once**;
- it must end up neither in the HTTP cache, nor in an application snapshot the system takes when it
  goes to the background — hence a `Cache-Control: no-store` response, and no key in a read model.

### 5.3 `HealthSample` — a time series

```
HealthSample
  run_id · measured_at (the measurement instant AT INGEST, not at reception)
  up_kbps · latency_ms · dropped_pct · jitter_ms · lost_packets · viewers
  source   ingest_server | client_submitted     ← two bitrates, two names
```

**The series is READABLE, not only pushed.** `realtime.md` §5.1 writes that "a bitrate curve is
re-requested": so it has to be requestable. A bounded read, a configurable window (default: three
minutes), carrying the samples **and the peak viewer count with its time** — which is derived from
the series and is obtainable only through it. Without that read, the curve and the peak are lost
after every `resume:too_old`, every reconnection, or simply when the console is opened in the middle
of a live show. **Served since 22 September 2026** as
`GET /v1/dates/{dateId}/run/health-samples` → `HealthSeries` (`realtime.md` §5.3).

**Every metric is nullable, and its absence means something** (`studio-web` Q16). Jitter and lost
packets **exist only on WebRTC ingest**; over RTMP on TCP they have no meaning, and the contract
**omits** them rather than serving zero. `studio-mobile` adds a third case the contract must carry:
*measured, but I did not receive it* — hence `measured_at` on each sample, which makes it possible to
show "bitrate 8.9 Mb/s, measured 3 s ago" or "last measurement 2 min ago" instead of "0 Mb/s", which
is a lie.

**End-to-end latency is a dedicated measurement** (`studio-web` Q17), never a native figure presented
as one. It is measured **on the run desk's client** by `RTCPeerConnection.getStats()` on the WHEP
path, and **submitted** to the server — hence `source: client_submitted`. If it is not measured, it
is **absent**. And **two different bitrates never carry the same name** (`studio-mobile`
inconsistency 7): `ingest_up_kbps` (a server observation) and `device_up_kbps` (a measurement of the
phone's uplink, which is not the encoder). Only the first feeds the pre-flight checklist.

**The monitor return path is served in the run's state** (`studio-web` Q18): WHEP under a second on
WHIP ingest, LL-HLS at a few seconds on RTMP ingest. The studio must **know** it so as not to
promise the operator a latency it does not have. `streaming.md` refuses to create a media branch
just to make a schema uniform: so the contract carries the truth, not uniformity.

### 5.4 `PlaybackSession` — root aggregate (the lease)

```
PlaybackSession
  id · account_id · profile_id · device_id · date_id
  state        active | released | expired | revoked
  lease_expires_at    now + 90 s, renewed by the token renewal
  quality_cap · drm_system · protocol
  opened_at · last_renewed_at
```

**It is the lease that carries the concurrent-screen limit, not a release command.**
`releasePlayback` cannot be guaranteed: a television gets unplugged, a set-top box loses power, the
system kills a mobile application without warning. A session that only closes on a client event
leaves a ghost screen, and the user is refused their own second playback. The answers to
`storefront-tv` Q9(c) and `storefront-mobile` Q5: **server-side expiry mandatory**, and the client
can **resume its own session**, identified by the device.

### 5.5 `ReplayAsset`, `PreviewBudget`, `ResumePoint`

`ReplayAsset`: existence, duration, `available_from`, **`expires_at` computed** from the end of the
run and `replay_window_hours` served by `catalog`. The **policy** belongs to `catalog`; the **file**
and its expiry belong to `streaming`. `RecordingProvider` stores and deletes; it is `@arthome/core`
that decides the duration, otherwise the replay policy would end up encoded in a storage lifecycle,
out of reach of the tests.

**Record the master feed at ingest**, not only the HLS variants: that way the replays can be cleanly
regenerated.

`PreviewBudget`: `(account_id, date_id) → seconds_used`. **Counted down server-side**
(`storefront-web` Q20, `storefront-mobile` Q6): a preview you extend by reloading the page is not a
preview, and a reinstalled application would reset a client-side counter to zero. The scope is **the
account, not the device** — otherwise a household with four devices gets four previews.

`ResumePoint`: `(profile_id, date_id) → position_sec`, `written_at`, `device_id`.
**Last writer wins, and the rank comes from the server** — a version number, never a date from the
client. The cadence the contract asks for: **on pause, on exit, on end, and a heartbeat every 30 to
60 s**, plus a **forced write when going to the background**. The contract accepts a **late write**:
the last position must be taken even if it arrives after a `releasePlayback` — a TV can be cut off at
any moment.
This is **not** a money command: it does not go through the strict idempotence regime, otherwise the
key becomes a cost per minute of playback per viewer.

### 5.6 `Incident` — cause and outcome are two vocabularies

`catalogue.incidentMessages` carries four entries that are **outcomes**, not causes. The mobile run
desk tells three more apart that exist nowhere. The contract separates them:

| `IncidentCause` (new) | `IncidentKind` (the outcome the viewer sees) |
|---|---|
| `venue_feed_lost` · `run_desk_disconnected` · `bitrate_collapsed` · `compatibility_worker_failed` · `provider_error` · `manual` | `hold_screen` · `postponed` · `cancelled` · `interrupted` |

The answer to `studio-mobile` Q12. And the **automatic triggering of the standby screen** — the
channel rule "if the feed is lost for more than 15 s" — produces **an incident of the same nature as
a manual trigger**, with `cause = venue_feed_lost` and `triggered_by = 'auto'`. That is the right
answer to the "the run-desk operator is unreachable" case, and it is a server rule, not an
application behaviour.

**The standby-screen message is content, not an i18n key** (`studio-web` Q19): it is written by the
run desk, it travels **with the language it was written in**, like a synopsis. It is the one assumed
exception to "i18n by codes", together with the inbox's texts. The catalogue supplies **templates**
per incident nature, which the run desk can keep or replace.

## 6. `chat`, `payouts`, `notifications` — the essentials

### 6.1 `chat`

```
ChatMessage
  id · date_id · channel_id · audience_member_id · author_handle · author_role
  at_media_sec   int   ← POSITION IN THE MEDIA, not the time it was sent
  sent_at        timestamptz
  seq            bigint, monotonic per date  ← the stream's resume point
  state          published | removed
  content_language · text
```

**The media anchor is the context's most unrecoverable decision** (`streaming.md` §5). Without it,
chat replayed over a replay is offset by however long the viewer took to start playback. Both are
carried — `at_media_sec` **and** `sent_at` — because the studio timestamps in venue time and the
moderation journal needs it.

`ModerationItem`: `state` (`reported | claimed | settled`), `reason`, `reports_count`, `claimed_by`
+ **`claim_expires_at`** (a short lease: a moderator who closes their browser does not freeze a row
for the whole live show), `verdict`, `settled_by`, `settled_at`, `origin`, `confirmed_by`.
**The second verdict is refused and carries the winning decision** — author and verdict — so the
screen shows "X has already deleted this message" instead of a bare failure. The answers to
`studio-web` Q21, Q22 and `studio-mobile` Q6.

#### Two counters, because the lease and the decision are not the same fact

`studio-mobile` C3 demonstrated, on the contract's own examples, that **a single counter cancels the
offline queue** — the one concession granted to mobile:

```
claim    → version 1 → 2      claim it
release  → version 2 → 3      release it, WITHOUT SETTLING ANYTHING
```

A moderator reads the queue at `version: 1`, loses the network, settles; their verdict goes into the
queue with `expectedVersion: 1`. A colleague claims then releases the row **with no verdict**. On
reconnection, the legitimate verdict is **refused**. On a live show at 60 messages a minute, rows
change lease constantly.

And the defect is deeper than a misplaced counter: the real rule is a **supersession** — *"claiming
is not settling: as long as your colleague has returned no verdict, your sanction applies"*. So a
verdict must be **accepted while another person holds the lease**. **A single counter cannot express
"refuse if settled, accept if merely claimed".**

> **`version` carries the lease. `decision_version` carries the settlement, and only a verdict
> increments it.** A verdict command is conditioned on `decision_version`, never on `version`.

It is the three-axis doctrine, applied once more: we had separated the **states**, and were still
stacking the **counters**.

#### Automatic moderation: nothing to build, a shape not to close

It is on the roadmap "in time". Three constraints, to be written now because they are free today and
expensive later — this is **forward compatibility**, not a feature.

1. **An automatic moderator takes no lease.** It acts at **ingestion**, not after a report. That is
   one more argument for the cut above: with a single counter, an automatic decision and a human
   verdict queued offline would collide in a way that counter **cannot express**.
2. **The precedence is written in one direction only: a human overturns an automatic decision, never
   the reverse.** Without that rule, a retroactive filter would erase a judgement already made — and
   human judgement is precisely what we keep for 24 months and journal by name.
3. **The origin survives the settlement.** The journal must be able to say "removed by the filter,
   **confirmed by X**" just as well as "removed by X". Hence `origin` **and** `confirmed_by` on the
   settled row: if one overwrites the other, we lose what it takes to measure the filter's quality,
   and that is unrecoverable. The same reasoning as a message's media anchor.

**A non-human actor requires no new shape**: `common.Actor` already carries `SURFACE_SYSTEM` (with an
empty `account_id`), which also serves the automatic standby screen and a lease expiring. A verdict
returned by a machine is an `Actor` like any other.

`AudienceMember` is **indexed by `(channel_id, account_id)`**, never by account alone: the same
person is a **distinct** member of the audience **in each channel**, with their own sanction, their
own history and their own visible nickname. That is what makes the isolation verifiable rather than
declarative — a freelance moderator working for three artists cannot read a viewer's history at the
fourth, because the row does not exist in their channel.

`AudienceMember`: a channel's audience is **a collection queryable in its own right**, not a
projection of the chat — the console looks for "a viewer present, who has not written"
(`studio-web` Q24). Nickname, sanction (`none | muted(expires_at) | banned`), dates followed,
message count, seniority, subscriber tier, **presence** on the live show in progress.

**A sanction carries an expiry instant** (nullable for "no limit"), never a label: 1 min, 10 min, 1 h
and a free-form duration are instants computed once.

`BannedWord`: adding a word **live** reclassifies messages already published if the retroactive
option is on. **The contract settles the ambiguity `studio-mobile` flags**: the processing is
**asynchronous**, and the command answers immediately with `reprocessing: true` and the **estimated**
number of messages concerned; the new queue items arrive over the realtime channel, marked
`origin: 'retroactive_filter'` so the journal can tell them apart from a human decision. Reason: a
synchronous reclassification over thousands of messages would block the command mid-show.

**The chat rate is measured by the server, in a declared unit** (`studio-mobile` #6, E15): a
**60-second** sliding window, the unit **messages per minute**, refreshed every **5 s**. The
console's switching threshold (60 msg/min) is a domain rule and reads against **that** measurement —
not against "number of messages ÷ hours elapsed", which is not the same thing.

### 6.2 `payouts`

```
PayoutLine
  id · channel_id · date_id · currency
  gross_ttc_minor
  vat_breakdown[]     { jurisdiction_code, jurisdiction_level, supply_kind,
                        rate_bps, base_minor, amount_minor }   ← breakdown PER JURISDICTION
                        rate_bps = the rate APPLIED AT THE SALE, never the current rate
  gross_ht_minor      = gross_ttc − Σ vat.amount
  commission_rate     SERVED, never re-derived from commission / gross
  commission_minor    = roundMinor(gross_ht × rate)
  net_minor           = gross_ht − commission
  refunded_minor · credited_minor
  state               scheduled | held | paid | refunded | suspended
  due_at              end of the live show + 14 days
  stripe_transfer_id · reconciled_at · discrepancy_minor
```

**The breakdown is the safe shape — but PER JURISDICTION, not per market.** `studio-web` found that
the payouts screen breaks VAT down "by country of purchase" while the fixture applies a single rate
to the gross. Both are wrong in one way or another, and **a billing market was never the right key**:
it is a **pricing** notion — which currency we sell in — not a **tax** notion. The country is not
enough either: roughly **9,000 jurisdictions** in the United States, and a rate that depends on the
pair **jurisdiction × nature of the supply** in the United Kingdom. See `adr-payments.md` §5.0, which
carries the full shape and the location evidence.

The breakdown stays correct **whatever the tax model**: a single-rate model produces a one-line
breakdown. The model is adopted (D-015); its legal validation is not.

**The commission rate is served**, not re-derived from `commission / gross` — the mockup re-derives
it and falls back to 12% by default when the gross is zero.

**`state` is driven by `outcome`**: `held` while an outcome is open (postponed, interrupted),
`refunded` if the date is cancelled, `suspended` while a change of bank details awaits its
counter-signature.

`BankAccountChangeRequest` — a full aggregate, **not a field** (`studio-web` Q11): two actors, two
distinct roles (owner **and** treasury), a deadline, a trace, and it **suspends the transfer in
progress** for the duration of the signature. A single write cannot carry that.

`ReconciliationPeriod`: does not close with an unexplained gap. `AccountingExport`: an asynchronous
job (BullMQ **internal to `payouts`**) returning a **short-lived signed URL**, usable **without a
session cookie** — an export protected by a cookie is undownloadable on the native shell
(`studio-mobile` Q9).

**The bounds of a "season"** (`studio-web` Q12) are a domain notion: `seasonBounds(date)` in
`@arthome/core`, **1 September → 31 August**, the live-performance convention. Served, never guessed
by five surfaces.

### 6.3 `notifications`

`PushRegistration` (device × account × platform × token × language), `NotificationPreference`
(5 triggers × 3 channels), `QuietHours` (23:00 → 09:00, **with the exception conditioned on holding
a seat** — that is a business rule, not an interface setting), `Reminder` (**dated: if the date is
postponed the reminder follows, if it is cancelled the reminder is cancelled and not fired into the
void**), `InboxEntry`, `AlertRoute` (role × channel → recipients, decided server-side; the
application does not filter a common feed).

**The five thresholds are served, and two of them had no owner.** "30 minutes before", "85% of the
seats" and "6 hours before expiry" are served among the storefront's constants. **The
moderation-queue threshold (10 messages) and the crew-assignment deadline (D-1) were nowhere** — two
constants named as domain rules and never carried, which is critical rule 15. They join the studio's
constants, beside the provisioning threshold, the revision deadline and the chat rate threshold.

**The preview budget's total is missing too** (`storefront-web` ❻.3): the contract serves the
**remainder** (`previewSecondsLeft`) and never the **ceiling**. But `decideWatch` needs it as an
input, and a surface that wants to show "4 min 12 of 5 min" cannot. The ceiling is a served domain
constant, like the others.

**The third channel, which nobody names** (`storefront-mobile` inconsistency 8, E15): the grid offers
three channels, only two are named (`push`, `email`), and the phone field carries the note "for
reminder SMS". **I propose `in_app`** and not `sms`. Reason: an SMS channel has a per-message cost, a
regulation of its own (consent, hours, opt-out) and one more provider, for a value nothing has
tested. `in_app` is free, already served by the existing notification centre, and promises nothing we
cannot keep. **To be confirmed by the chief**: it is a proposal, not an observation.

## 7. Persistence

### 7.1 Identifiers (C5)

**UUIDv7 generated in the domain**, by `@arthome/core`, **never by a database default**. Three
reasons, two of which are expressed needs:

1. **The outbox requires it**: the aggregate must know its identifier before insertion, since the
   outbox row carries `aggregateid` and is written in the same transaction.
2. **The studio requires it** (`studio-web` Q27): the `wizard` announces "draft saved" before any
   round trip to the server. An identifier generated by the domain makes it possible to create, save
   locally and synchronise with no reconciliation. A database default would impose one more
   correlation key.
3. UUIDv7's temporal ordering gives well-filled B-tree indexes, which a v4 does not.

**A UUIDv7 reveals its creation date**, and a rule has to be drawn from that rather than a note.

| Kind of object | Internal identifier | Exposed identifier |
|---|---|---|
| date, show, artist, venue | UUIDv7 | **the same**, plus a `slug` — a date's creation date is not a secret |
| account, profile, person | UUIDv7, **never exposed** | `public_handle` (`@marie.j`), opaque and chosen |
| playback session, pairing, token | UUIDv7 **internal** | a distinct opaque identifier, random — otherwise the instant a pairing opened leaks and helps guess the code |
| order, invoice | UUIDv7 | a **readable reference** (`ATH-2026-00042`), which is also what support reads over the phone |

PostgreSQL 18's native `uuidv7()` stays available as a **fallback default** on the technical tables
(outbox, journals) where no domain pre-generates.

### 7.2 One database per service, and migrations per service

- **Seven PostgreSQL 18 databases, one per service.** No service reads another's database, no CDC
  connector crosses a context boundary.
- **Migrations per service, never a shared runner.** Each service has its own `DataSource`, its own
  migrations, its own history.
- **Run by a dedicated job, never at application startup.** `migrationsRun: true` would migrate N
  replicas at the same time, and TypeORM has no migration lock. The job is a distinct deployment
  step, which runs **once**, as a single instance, and must succeed before the new version starts.
- **On compiled JavaScript** (`typeorm migration:run -d dist/data-source.js`): `tsx` does not supply
  decorator metadata and produces `ColumnTypeUndefinedError`.
- `synchronize: true` is forbidden everywhere, including in shared development.

### 7.3 The outbox table — the contract between TypeORM and the Debezium router

One per service, the same shape everywhere. It is an infrastructure contract: the column names are
the ones the *Debezium Outbox Event Router* expects.

```sql
CREATE TABLE outbox_event (
  id              uuid        PRIMARY KEY,              -- UUIDv7, becomes the message-id
  aggregatetype   text        NOT NULL,                 -- "catalog.date" → Kafka topic name
  aggregateid     text        NOT NULL,                 -- → Kafka partition KEY
  type            text        NOT NULL,                 -- "catalog.date.scheduled.v1" → header
  payload         bytea       NOT NULL,                 -- Protobuf ALREADY framed for the registry
  tracecontext    text        NULL,                     -- W3C traceparent, injected at write time
  actor_id        text        NULL,                     -- by name, required by the studio journal
  created_at      timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()
);
```

**Three points that make the difference between "it works" and "it works in production".**

1. **The payload is in wire format — as a TARGET. Today it is bare Protobuf, and the gap is named
   here rather than discovered by a consumer.** The intent stands: the producer serialises with the
   registry serialiser from `@arthome/contracts`, so the bytes written carry the magic byte, the
   schema identifier and the message indexes; Debezium merely transports them
   (`binary.handling.mode=bytes`, `value.converter=ByteArrayConverter`), and without that framing
   Debezium would produce framed JSON no Protobuf consumer could read.

   **What is written today is `toBinary(…)` and nothing in front of it**, in both producers that
   exist — `identity`'s `register-account.service.ts` and `catalog`'s `publish-show.service.ts` —
   each carrying a comment saying the framing "belongs in front of these bytes and arrives with the
   schema registry". There is no registry in the stack yet.

   **Why this paragraph is dangerous rather than merely early.** A consumer written from the
   sentence above strips a prefix that is not there, hands the remainder to `fromBinary`, and fails
   to decode — and undecodable bytes are classified `PermanentError`, which means the message does
   **not** retry. It **dead-letters on the first attempt**. That is the right handling of bytes that
   really are malformed and the worst available outcome for bytes that were fine, and the symptom
   an operator sees is a dead-letter reason saying the payload is not the schema. The one consumer
   built, `search-indexer`, decodes bare and therefore works; the next one written from this
   paragraph will not. Switching framing on is additive for a producer and **breaking for every
   consumer**: both sides move in one step, or the topic is versioned.

2. **Nothing is ever updated or read by the application.** The application **only inserts**, inside
   the business transaction. It is CDC that reads the WAL.

   **`REPLICA IDENTITY DEFAULT` is enough — and the reason is the primary key, not the absence of
   deletes.** The reason recorded here used to be "since there is neither an `UPDATE` nor a `DELETE`
   captured". Two independent facts falsify that premise while leaving the conclusion standing, and
   both are worth knowing on their own:

   - **The publication was created `FOR ALL TABLES`.** `publication.autocreate.mode` is not a
     property you get by default in the shape you want: its default is `all_tables`, so Debezium
     runs `CREATE PUBLICATION … FOR ALL TABLES` when the publication does not exist.
     `table.include.list` then filters **at the connector, after logical decoding** — the other
     tables' rows are read off the WAL and shipped into the Connect worker before being discarded.
     Measured on the running stack on 2026-09-25: `puballtables = t`, the publication carrying
     `account`, `migrations` **and** `outbox_event`, so every `account` `UPDATE` was in fact being
     captured. The connector now sets `"publication.autocreate.mode": "filtered"`;
     `arthome-platform/infra/debezium/README.md` holds the query that settles it and the superuser
     consequence (§7.4).
   - **Point 3 below mandates `DELETE FROM outbox_event …`** — on the very table this point says
     has no `DELETE` captured. The two points contradicted each other before any publication was
     created, and no infrastructure detail is needed to see it.

   **So the condition, not the accident**: `DEFAULT` uses the table's primary key as its replica
   identity, so it is sufficient for **any captured table that has one**, whatever is done to that
   table. All three the over-broad publication carried do — `account.id` and `outbox_event.id` are
   `uuid PRIMARY KEY`, and TypeORM's `migrations.id` is a generated primary key — which is why
   nothing broke while the premise was false. A captured table **without** a primary key is where
   `FULL` becomes necessary and expensive (§7.4), and scoping the publication is what stops that
   from being decided by accident.

   **What happens on the first purge is not settled, and saying so is the point.** Point 3's
   cleanup job deletes from a captured table; the connector configures **no delete handling of any
   kind**. So the Outbox Event Router's behaviour on a `DELETE` is **undefined by the configuration
   as written** — which is not the same as known to be harmless. It has never run, because the job
   does not exist yet. Settle it against Debezium's own documentation, and settle it **before the
   job is written**. Left unsettled, the answer is discovered on the first purge, in production, on
   the one connector whose stopping retains the write-ahead log until a disk fills (§7.4).

3. **Cleanup is a separate job** — `DELETE FROM outbox_event WHERE created_at < now() - interval
   '7 days'`, run by a task of the service. It must run **after** the connector has confirmed its
   position, otherwise we delete rows not yet published.

**And the rule that makes all this safe**: the business write and the outbox row are in the **same
transaction**, through the **same `manager`**. Never `save()` then `emit()`: a crash between the two
loses the event, a rollback after the emission invents it.

### 7.4 What CDC imposes on migrations

This is the point where an innocuous migration breaks production, and it deserves to be written
explicitly into `critical-rules.md`.

| Constraint | Practical consequence |
|---|---|
| `wal_level = logical` | a server parameter, a restart required; to be set at milestone 2 |
| **one replication slot and one publication per connector**, and the publication **scoped to that service's `outbox_event` alone** | seven Debezium connectors, seven slots, seven publications, named `arthome_<service>_outbox`. **Naming a publication is not scoping it**: `publication.autocreate.mode` defaults to `all_tables`, so Debezium creates it `FOR ALL TABLES` unless you set `filtered`, and `table.include.list` filters afterwards, at the connector. Verify on the running stack — `SELECT * FROM pg_publication_tables WHERE pubname = 'arthome_<service>_outbox'` should return exactly one row, `public.outbox_event`; more than one, or `puballtables = t` in `pg_publication`, means the default won. `FOR ALL TABLES` **also requires superuser**, which is why it passes in development — the image's `POSTGRES_USER` is one — and fails on the first real deployment, where the tempting repair is to grant superuser to a connector user |
| an unconsumed slot **retains the WAL** | a stopped connector makes the disk grow until it is full. **Measure: `confirmed_flush_lsn` lag > 1 GB → alert**, and never a slot left behind after a test |
| `REPLICA IDENTITY` | `DEFAULT` on `outbox_event` is enough — **because it has a primary key**, not because only inserts are captured (§7.3 point 2 corrects that reason). For a captured table **with no primary key**, `FULL` would be necessary and expensive |
| **renaming a column breaks replication** | the publication references the columns; the connector fails or loses the column in silence |

**The rule that follows, and it is not negotiable on `outbox_event`**:

> **Additive migrations only on captured tables.** A rename happens in four steps: add the column,
> backfill, write to both, then drop the old one **in a later version**, after checking the
> connector's position.

The same discipline holds for indexes: `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` outside a transaction, hence
outside TypeORM's transaction mechanism — a dedicated migration, marked as such.

### 7.5 Retention and erasure

| Data | Retention | Erasure mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **`outbox_event`** (every publishing service) | **7 days** (§7.3 point 3) | `DELETE FROM outbox_event WHERE created_at < now() - interval '7 days'`, a job of the service. **Gated on the connector, not on the clock**: it may only delete what the connector has confirmed, and `confirmed_flush_lsn` (§7.4) is the position to read. Deleting ahead of it destroys a committed business fact that was never published — and the application never reads this table back, so nothing notices. It also deletes from a **captured** table: see §7.3 point 2 for what the connector does not configure |
| **`processed_message`** (every consuming service) | **owed, and not set** | the deduplication ledger — `id`, `topic`, `processed_at`. There is **no purge today** and it grows with every message ever consumed. Its horizon must **outlive the retry-plus-DLQ budget**: 5 s, 30 s then 5 min between attempts (`libs/messaging`), plus however long a dead-lettered message may sit before somebody replays it. Purged sooner, it stops deduplicating precisely the replays it exists for — the message returns, finds no row, and is applied a second time, silently |
| chat messages | **24 months** (aligned on the studio journal) | a monthly purge per date partition; the message is deleted, the **moderation journal entry** stays with the message's identifier and not its text |
| studio journal, access journal — the **`identity.channel_journal` table** (§4) | **24 months** | a purge by period; an export before the purge |
| health samples | **90 days** in detail, hourly aggregates kept | a table partitioned by month, `DROP PARTITION` |
| resume points | 24 months with no read | purge |
| playback sessions | 30 days | purge |
| pairings | 7 days after the outcome | purge |
| **invoices** | **10 years** | never deleted — an accounting obligation |
| **an order's tax location and its evidence** | **10 years** | never deleted nor anonymised: they are **six mandatory items** — date, location evidence, nature of the product, applicable rate, VAT amount, total. An IP address is kept there **as tax evidence**, which is a legal basis distinct from consent and must be declared as such |
| payout lines, accounting entries | **10 years** | same |
| a deleted account | **anonymised, not deleted** | see below |

**Deleting an account is a financial command as much as a personal one** (`storefront-web` Q26). The
copy says: *"Deletion cancels unused seats."* So it triggers refunds, it touches artists' payouts
that may already be computed, and it runs into the ten-year accounting retention. It **cannot be
synchronous and cannot be total.**

The sequence, and it is a persistent saga flow, not a call:

1. `identity` moves the account to `deletion_requested` and publishes
   `identity.account.deletion_requested.v1`. Sign-ins are blocked, **the account is not yet erased**.
2. `ticketing` cancels unused seats, refunds according to the policy, closes the subscription at term,
   and publishes its acknowledgement. `payouts` recomputes the lines touched.
3. A **30-day grace period** runs. The account is reactivable by simply signing in during that period
   — that is what makes the irreversible acceptable.
4. At term: `identity` **anonymises** — `email`, `phone`, `name`, `public_handle`, avatars, devices,
   IP addresses and chat nicknames replaced by non-reversible values; `status = 'anonymised'`. The
   **invoices keep their frozen content** (an invoice is a document, the one assumed exception to
   "never a formatted string transported"), and the accounting lines keep an anonymised account
   identifier.
5. The **chat messages** are dissociated from the person (`audience_member_id` set to null, nickname
   replaced) but **not deleted** while their 24-month retention runs: they are part of a by-name
   moderation journal an artist may need.

**Exports (data, invoices) are asynchronous** (`storefront-web` Q27): the command returns an
acknowledgement and a request identifier, the state is queryable, and the document arrives through a
**short-lived signed URL**. A tax export or a GDPR export is not an HTTP response.

## 8. What a response always carries

A summary, for `backend-contracts`.

```
servedAt        the server instant — EVERY countdown refers to it, never to the client's clock
validUntil      present as soon as a perishable value is in the response
version         on every aggregate a conditional command may target
lastEventSeq    on every read model fed by a stream, for the resume
```

and, on every error:

```
code            a closed vocabulary, i18n by codes
params          the message's parameters (never the sentence)
traceId         readable and copyable from the error screen — on mobile it is the only link
                between "my application crashed" and a server log
nature          refused | unavailable | offline_forbidden
```
